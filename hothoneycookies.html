Hot honey cookies

Last made September 26th 2026 at 22:00 by Kyu

Jump to recipe | Jump to story

Ingredients

Dry ingredients

  • 3 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon baking soda

Wet ingredients

  • 2 eggs (room temperature)
  • 1 cup butter (softened)
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup hot honey
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

Equipment

  • Small bowl
  • Medium bowl
  • Whisk
  • Stand mixer with a flat beater attachment
  • Spatula
  • Fridge
  • Baking sheet
  • Parchment paper
  • Oven at 350°F
  • Circle cookie cutter
  • Wire rack

Instructions

Preparation

  1. Crack open 2 eggs into small bowl to bring to room temperature.
  2. Take out 1 cup butter to soften.
  3. Whisk 3 cups all-purpose flour, 1 teaspoon baking powder, and 1/4 teaspoon baking soda into dry ingredients bowl.

Cookie dough

  1. Add butter, 1 cup granulated sugar, 1/2 cup hot honey, and 1/2 teaspoon salt into the stand mixer bowl with a flat beater attachment.
  2. Set stand mixer to medium speed (4-6) and let it run for ~1 minute or until the ingredients are creamy and well combined.
  3. Add in eggs and 1 teaspoon vanilla extract.
  4. Set the stand mixer to medium speed (4-6) and let it run until well combined.

  5. While the stand mixer is set to low speed (2-4) and until dough is well-mixed:

    • Gradually add the contents of dry ingredients bowl into the stand mixer bowl.
    • Periodically scrape down the sides of the stand mixer bowl with a spatula.
  6. Cover dough and refrigerate for at least 1 hour.

Baking

  1. Line 1 baking sheet with parchment paper.
  2. Preheat the oven to 350°F.

  3. Form the cookie dough into loosely packed balls about 1 1/2 tablespoon in size and place 2 inches apart on the baking sheets.

    • Use a cookie scoop to help.
    • Be able to place cookies in at least a 5x3 grid.
    • Refrigerate any extra dough to repeat this process later.

  4. Bake for ~10 minutes or until the edges start to brown.

    • Shape with circle cookie cutter afterwards, while still hot.
  5. Let cool on baking sheet for ~5 minutes.
  6. Transfer to wire rack and let cool completely.

Behold. Known by my friends as "chud son cookies," they're supposed to have a face made out of icing, but:

  • I was too lazy
  • I was in a time crunch (an event I was making these for was happening the day after)
  • I didn't even have the right cookie icing (I had a gel, which wouldn't fit what I wanted to do).

Anyway, I was given a task by a friend once to make honey cookies but use hot honey. The thought of having spicy cookies intrigued me, so I made them.

Hot honey on its own is interesting. It's sweet, sure, but then the heat creeps up on you after a while. I was lowkey excited to have spicy cookies. Cookies that would make a Victorian-era child perish.

Unfortunately, the resulting cookies weren't spicy at all. I do want to add onto this recipe in the future. Maybe cinnamon?

The reason why they're affectionately called chud son cookies is because they're round and yellow, like Bbmarutchi from Tamagotchi, shown on the right. If I had the correct cookie icing, I would draw the face, but I didn't, so the cookies are faceless for now.
